03

Scoring system

Public federal data, built the way commercial flood and wind models are weighted

What goes into the score
  • Coastal floodingNRI coastal flood risk: storm surge and tidal flooding frequency times the value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally.
  • HurricaneNRI hurricane risk: modeled wind loss to buildings. Every Maryland ZIP carries some hurricane score; the Shore carries the most.
  • Inland floodingNRI riverine and inland flood risk: it raises scores along rivers and urban streams, but no Maryland ZIP reaches High on inland flooding alone.
  • ResilienceNRI community resilience: local capacity to recover, used as a small adjustment.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (flood zone determinations, wind pools) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same ingredients.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeCoastal flood score 95+, or 50%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for coastal flooding
  • HighCoastal flood score 85+, 20%+ of residents in high coastal-flood tracts, or inland flood score 95+
  • ModerateCoastal flood score 50+, inland flood score 85+, hurricane score 90+, or any single tract with a coastal flood score of 99+
  • LowEverything else
Reference points

Flood as a cross-sell

Homeowners policies exclude flood; the coastal ZIPs are where it sells

Why it matters here
  • Coverage gapStandard homeowners policies do not cover flood. In High and Extreme ZIPs a flood quote belongs in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • Ocean CityThe town takes part in FEMA's Community Rating System, which lowers NFIP flood premiums for residents. Confirm the current class when you quote.
  • HurricaneEvery Lower Shore ZIP carries a hurricane score of 89 or higher out of 100; wind and hurricane deductibles are worth explaining at quote time.
  • Second homes82% of Ocean City's 32,621 homes were vacant at survey time, most of them seasonal. Condo and second-home owners are a flood audience that rarely shows up as a Home lead.
